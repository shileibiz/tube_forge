[BULD SECTION - 0:45-1:45]
Here's the stack I used — every single tool is free or has a generous free tier.

First, RSS feeds and web scrapers to pull raw content. I used a Python script with Feedparser and Requests — zero cost. Every morning, it pulls the latest from thirty RSS feeds across tech, AI, and startup news.

Second, an LLM layer to summarize and rank. I used Claude's API for the heavy lifting — the prompt is simple: "Read this article, summarize in two sentences, and rate its relevance to builders on a scale of one to ten." Cost? About three cents per hundred articles.

Third, a local vector database to track what I've already covered. ChromaDB is free and runs on my laptop. No duplicate content, no rehashing old topics.

Fourth, a simple newsletter generator that formats everything into a clean HTML template. I used Jinja2 — again, free and Python-native.

[TECHNICAL DEEP DIVE - 1:45-2:45]
Let me show you the actual architecture. The pipeline runs on a cron job every morning at 6 AM. A Python script fetches content, sends it through the LLM for analysis, stores embeddings in ChromaDB, and outputs a Markdown file with the top ten pieces of content ranked by relevance.

The trick is the ranking system. I don't just use the LLM scores. I cross-reference with engagement data — tweets that got saved, articles that trended on Hacker News, GitHub repos that gained stars overnight. Each signal is weighted. The final score is a weighted average.

Here's the real magic: I fine-tuned the scoring weights over eight weeks. Initially, the LLM score was weighted at sixty percent. After testing, I found that Hacker News engagement was a better predictor of what my audience actually cared about. Now the weights are: forty percent HN points, thirty percent LLM relevance, twenty percent GitHub stars, ten percent Twitter saves.

[LEARNINGS - 3:30-4:15]
Three things I learned that might save you months of trial and error.

One: Don't over-engineer the first version. My first prototype was forty lines of Python. It worked. I spent weeks trying to make it perfect before I realized perfect doesn't need to exist, it needs to ship.

Two: Raw data quality matters more than model quality. I was chasing better summarization models, but the real bottleneck was bad RSS feeds with incomplete content. Cleaning the data pipeline gave me a fifty percent improvement in output quality.

Three: The scoring system needs real-world validation. Your model's opinion on what's relevant doesn't matter. What matters is what your audience actually engages with. Use real metrics, not vibes.
